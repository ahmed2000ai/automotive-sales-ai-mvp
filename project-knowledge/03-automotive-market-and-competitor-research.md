# Automotive Market and Competitor Research

**File:** `03-automotive-market-and-competitor-research.md`  
**Purpose:** Document the premium automotive protection competitor landscape across Riyadh, Jeddah, and the Eastern Province, and map the specific sales-process gaps that support the MVP hypothesis.

---

## 1. Research Objective

The goal of this research was to identify approximately 30 actual Saudi businesses offering:

- PPF
- ceramic coating
- tinting
- wrapping
- premium detailing

across:

- Riyadh
- Jeddah
- Eastern Province

The research examined, where publicly visible:

- websites
- WhatsApp entry points
- Instagram / TikTok / Snapchat presence
- online booking
- visible CRM / software behavior
- reviews
- pricing
- customer journey maturity

The purpose was not simply to build a directory.

The real objective was:

> **Find which parts of the sales process are already well-served and which gaps remain commercially valuable.**

---

## 2. High-Level Market Finding

The sector appears:

- fragmented
- reputation-driven
- heavily dependent on Google reviews
- strongly influenced by social media
- WhatsApp-centric
- uneven in digital maturity

Some operators already have:

- polished websites
- online booking
- clear packages
- financing options
- loyalty programs
- warranty systems
- e-commerce checkout

Others still rely mainly on:

- WhatsApp
- phone
- Linktree
- Google Maps
- social DMs

This fragmentation is favorable for a standardized revenue-automation product.

# 6. Major Competitive Finding

The market already contains many examples of:

- online booking
- package pricing
- warranties
- loyalty
- installment payments
- e-commerce
- WhatsApp buttons
- social links
- service catalogs

Therefore:

> **None of these should be the main product differentiator.**

The stronger opportunity appears to be the layer that manages what happens **after the enquiry arrives**.

---

# 7. Feature Gaps Identified

The research suggested the following unmet or underexposed needs.

## 7.1 Every Enquiry Becomes a Lead

All inbound sources should create a structured opportunity:

- WhatsApp
- Instagram
- TikTok
- Snapchat
- Meta Lead Ads
- Google Ads
- website
- phone / call source

---

## 7.2 Automatic Vehicle / Service Qualification

Fields may include:

- make
- model
- year
- service
- full / partial PPF
- gloss / matte
- film preference
- desired date
- branch
- budget

---

## 7.3 Quote Pipeline

Suggested stages:

- New
- Qualified
- Quote Sent
- Follow-up
- Appointment
- Deposit
- Vehicle Received
- Completed
- Lost

---

## 7.4 Quote Recovery

If a customer receives a quotation and disappears:

- structured follow-up
- automatic reminders
- sales prompts
- escalation
- stop when the customer responds

This was initially considered the biggest feature.

Later mystery-shopping evidence refined the hypothesis:

> some businesses already follow up well, so the deeper problem is **inconsistent execution**.

---

## 7.5 Salesperson SLA

Example management alert:

> Salesperson has 11 enquiries unanswered for more than 15 minutes.

This became more important later when Liquidglass demonstrated the difference between instant automated acknowledgement and slow meaningful response.

---

## 7.6 Lead-Source Revenue Attribution

Desired output:

> Snapchat campaign -> leads -> bookings -> revenue

This was consistently identified as a higher-value management feature.

---

## 7.7 Lost-Reason Analytics

Possible reasons:

- price
- competitor
- no response
- timing
- location
- warranty
- financing
- no current need

---

## 7.8 Reactivation

Potential campaigns:

- PPF inspection
- ceramic refresh
- detailing
- tint replacement
- second vehicle
- old quotation

---

## 7.9 Review Automation

After successful service:

- send review request
- direct unhappy feedback to manager
- track review conversion

---

## 7.10 Referral Tracking

Potential second-stage moat:

**dealer / showroom / salesperson -> referred vehicle -> booking -> revenue -> referral fee**

This could become valuable because automotive-protection centers often receive business through vehicle sales relationships.

# 8. What Not to Build First

This research explicitly reduced the MVP scope.

Do not lead with:

- online booking
- generic pricing pages
- warranty verification
- loyalty
- POS
- accounting
- inventory
- workshop job cards

Reason:

> competitors already offer many of these functions.

---

# 9. Ideal Prospect Profile

Best early prospects appear to be:

- established but not enterprise
- roughly 150-700+ Google reviews
- significant social activity
- high-ticket PPF packages
- WhatsApp-heavy
- enough lead volume to show ROI
- weak or invisible sales-process infrastructure
- multiple salespeople or branches if possible

Examples identified as especially interesting to investigate further included:

- City Pro
- Liquidglass Protect
- NanoView
- Polish Engineer
- SunShield
- Clear Pro
- Pro Master
- Viper Smart
- PROF Car Care
- VIP Car Care

This does **not** mean these businesses are poorly run.

It means their public footprint suggested:

> enough lead volume and enough operational complexity for sales-conversion automation to matter.

---

# 10. Geographic Conclusion

The research supported:

> **Start in Jeddah for validation, then expand to Riyadh.**

Reason:

- sufficient market depth
- strong premium automotive presence
- easier local validation
- lower friction for direct meetings / pilots

Eastern Province remains attractive for later expansion.

---

# 11. Most Important Strategic Finding

The key conclusion from the competitor research was:

> **The opportunity is not another website, booking form, loyalty system, warranty system, or generic chatbot.**

The product should focus on:

> **lead understanding + salesperson discipline + complete answers + quote management + follow-up + management visibility + attribution**

This conclusion directly led to the WhatsApp mystery-shopping experiment.
